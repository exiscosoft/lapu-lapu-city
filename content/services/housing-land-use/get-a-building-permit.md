# Get a Building Permit — Lapu-Lapu City

You need a building permit from the Office of the Building Official (OBO) at the City Engineering Office before you build, renovate, repair, alter, move, or demolish a building or structure. This applies to developers, contractors, individual project owners, and government agencies.

---

## What You Need to Know

| Information         | Details                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Where**           | Office of the Building Official (OBO), City Engineering Office                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **When**            | Ask the office                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Requirements**    | Locational clearance, proof of your right over the lot, and signed and sealed plans (architectural, sanitary/plumbing, structural, electrical, electronics, mechanical)                                                           |
| **Cost**            | Building permit fees under the National Building Code fee schedule, computed by each section of the office. The charter does not list amounts. You pay at the City Treasurer's Office.                                            |
| **Processing Time** | The charter does not give a total. Most office steps take 3 to 15 minutes each, but you also need time to correct any deficiencies and to get a Fire Safety Evaluation Clearance (FSEC) from the Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP). |

**Why you need it:** The National Building Code of the Philippines (Presidential Decree 1096, Section 301) says no one may build, alter, repair, move, convert, or demolish a building or structure without first getting a building permit from the Building Official.

**Before you start:** Get your [locational clearance](/services/housing-land-use/request-zoning-land-use-or-building-permits) from the City Planning and Development Office first. After construction, you need an [occupancy permit](/services/housing-land-use/get-an-occupancy-permit) before you use the building.

---

## How to Apply

### Step 1: Get the forms and checklist

- Ask the receiving staff for the application forms and the checklist of requirements (about 5 minutes).

### Step 2: Submit your plans and documents

- Submit your plans, filled-in permit forms, and supporting documents (about 5 minutes).
- Once received, your plans and documents stay in the office. You can only take them out by withdrawing your application.

### Step 3: Wait for the technical review

- Each section (line and grade, architectural, sanitary/plumbing, electrical, electronics and communication, and mechanical) checks your documents against the checklist (about 10 minutes per section).
- If something is missing or wrong, the section head writes it on your checklist sheet.

### Step 4: Correct any deficiencies

- The receiving staff return your documents with the list of deficiencies.
- Correct your documents, add what is missing, and resubmit them to the section heads for re-checking.

### Step 5: Get your fees assessed

- Each section computes your permit fees, including any surcharges and penalties.
- The receiving staff prepare your collection sheet and tell you when it is ready (about 10 minutes).

### Step 6: Pay at the City Treasurer's Office

- Take your collection sheet to the City Treasurer's Office and pay the building permit fees.

### Step 7: Get your endorsement to the Bureau of Fire Protection

- Show your official receipt to the OBO. Staff prepare an endorsement letter to the BFP (about 15 minutes).
- Take the letter to the BFP and get your Fire Safety Evaluation Clearance (FSEC).

### Step 8: Submit your FSEC

- Submit the FSEC and the remaining copies of your documents. The Building Official reviews and signs your permit (about 30 minutes).

### Step 9: Claim your building permit

- Claim your building permit from the releasing staff (about 10 minutes).

## Requirements

The full checklist is in the Official Citizen's Charter section below. Here is a summary of what each professional prepares.

### Documents Needed

**Line and grade**

- [ ] Locational clearance - [City Planning and Development Office](/services/housing-land-use/request-zoning-land-use-or-building-permits)
- [ ] Height clearance, if your building is within the approach or departure zones of airport runways - Civil Aviation Authority of the Philippines (CAAP) / Mactan-Cebu International Airport Authority (MCIAA)
- [ ] If you own the lot: certified true copy of the land title (TCT) - Registry of Deeds; tax declaration - [City Assessor](/services/housing-land-use/request-property-records-from-the-assessor); current real property tax receipt - [City Treasurer's Office](/services/housing-land-use/pay-real-property-tax)
- [ ] If you don't own the lot: notarized contract of lease, deed of absolute sale, or contract of sale - lot owner
- [ ] Lot plan - licensed geodetic engineer
- [ ] Location plan and site development plan - licensed architect
- [ ] Barangay clearance - your barangay

**Architectural** (licensed architect)

- [ ] 5 sets of plans and specifications, signed and sealed by a licensed architect or civil engineer (with structural plans for buildings of 2 storeys and up)
- [ ] Floor plan, elevations, and sections (scale not less than 1:100), reflected ceiling plan, provisions for persons with disabilities (PWD), fire exit details for multi-dwelling and multi-use buildings, door and window schedule, and stair design
- [ ] Bill of materials and specifications, signed and sealed, at least 2 copies

**Sanitary and plumbing** (sanitary engineer or master plumber)

- [ ] Sanitary plan, isometric layout, septic tank detail, drainage riser diagram, design analysis, and cost estimate
- [ ] Plumbing application forms and worksheets, location plan, and lot plan showing the building and septic tank
- [ ] Certification from a registered master plumber, with photocopies of their Professional Regulation Commission (PRC) ID and Professional Tax Receipt (PTR)
- [ ] Contractor's tax paid at the City Treasurer's Office

**Structural** (civil or structural engineer)

- [ ] Design analysis and boring test for buildings of 3 storeys and up
- [ ] PRC ID and PTR of the designing engineer
- [ ] Foundation, floor framing, and roof framing plans (scale not less than 1:100), and footing, column, and structural member details

**Electrical** (professional electrical engineer)

- [ ] Electrical plan with location plan and riser diagram, application form and worksheet, bill of materials, design analysis, specifications, and the engineer's PRC ID and PTR

**Electronics** (professional electronics and communication engineer)

- [ ] Electronics plan with location plan, application form, bill of materials, specifications, and the engineer's PRC ID and PTR

**Mechanical** (professional mechanical engineer)

- [ ] Mechanical plans (location and key plan, general layout, design computations, machinery drawings, and where needed boilers, fire suppression, and duct work), application form, bill of materials, specifications, and the engineer's PTR

### Eligibility

- Developers
- Contractors
- Individual project proponents
- Government agencies

## Official Citizen's Charter

### Issuance of Building Permit (Including Renovation, Repair, and Structures)

The National Building Code of the Philippines

(Presidential Decree 1096), Chapter 3, Section 301

**Building Permits**

_"No person, firm or corporation, including any agency, or, instrumentality, of the government shall erect, construct, alter, repair, move, convert or demolish any building or structure or cause the same to be done without first obtaining a building permit therefor from the Building Official assigned in the place where the subject building is located or the building work is to be done."_

**Service**

Processing and approval of applications for construction, renovation, repair, or demolition of structures in compliance with the National Building Code of the Philippines (PD 1096).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do I need a building permit for repairs or renovation?**
A: Yes. The National Building Code requires a permit to erect, construct, alter, repair, move, convert, or demolish a building or structure.

**Q: Can I take my plans home while my application is being processed?**
A: No. Once received, plans and documents stay in the office unless you withdraw your application.

**Q: Where do I pay?**
A: At the City Treasurer's Office, using the collection sheet the OBO prepares for you.
